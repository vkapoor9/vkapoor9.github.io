## Why I built this

Buyers stopped typing keywords into a search box and started asking an assistant for a recommendation. A business can rank perfectly and still never be named. There was no way to check, so every conversation about it was conjecture.

## How it works

Two engines, deliberately independent.

- **Structural scanner.** Real network checks: robots.txt rules for eight AI crawlers, llms.txt, JSON-LD (LocalBusiness, Organization, FAQPage, Review), head hygiene, sitemap, FAQ and name/address/phone signals, HTTPS and response health. Runs with no API keys at all.
- **AI engine probe.** Asks 8 to 12 buyer-intent questions ("best plumber in [town]") across ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity, then measures how often the business and its named competitors come back. Needs keys, and skips cleanly without them rather than failing.
- **Batch mode.** Scans a CSV of prospects and returns a ranked list with factual opener lines drawn from what the scan actually found.
- **Output.** One self-contained HTML report.

## What it took

Zero runtime dependencies was a constraint I set on purpose and kept. It runs on a clean Node install with no lockfile drift, no install step, and nothing in the tree that I did not write. For a tool whose entire value is the credibility of its findings, a transitive dependency I have not read is a liability rather than a convenience.

Every check is a real request. Nothing is inferred from a cached third-party index, because an aggregator that lags by a month will confidently report a robots.txt rule that changed last week.

## What I learned

The hard part was not the probing. It was accepting that a claim made to a business about its own website is checkable by that business in about four seconds, so a false positive costs more than the entire report is worth. The test suite exists to protect against being confidently wrong in front of someone who knows better.
